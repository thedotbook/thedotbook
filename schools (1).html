The Dollar Book for Schools

Last updated: October 2026

The Dollar Book is a free, not-for-profit resource that makes large numbers and real-world money easier to picture. Every $ sign is $1 million; every page holds 3,000 of them; page 334 is $1 trillion. Pupils can find where the world’s wealthiest people sit, compare countries, and look up what 287 UK jobs pay before and after tax.

Use the school link

Show this on the board or put it in your lesson slides. Pupils scan the code or type the address:

thedollarbook.com/?school=1

The school link is remembered on that device. To go back to the full version, add ?school=0 to the address.

What school mode changes

The full version of the site has an optional AI assistant and a voice search button. School mode switches both off, along with a few other things, so nothing a pupil types is ever sent to an AI service.

  • No AI chat – the assistant, the “Ask about…” boxes and the AI name search are all removed. A name that isn’t in the book simply says so.
  • No voice input – the microphone button is removed (voice search sends audio to the browser maker’s speech service).
  • No live currency or crypto lookups – the book uses built-in exchange rates instead, so currency conversions are approximate.
  • No admin access from a school-mode device.
  • A banner at the top shows that school mode is on.

Everything else is unchanged: the wealth lists, the country lists and the search. The Career Guide is now its own page, with a country picker (UK and US so far), gross and take-home pay for every job, and the Tax tables. Please preview the whole book before you use it with a class. It includes sections on politics and religion, and some entries cover public figures’ wealth, so you will want to decide what suits your pupils.

Data protection and safeguarding at a glance

Accounts or log-insNone. Nobody signs up or gives a name or email.
Personal data collectedNone on purpose. See the privacy policy.
Advertising, tracking, analyticsNone. The site has no adverts and no analytics scripts.
Stored on the pupil’s own deviceOnly if they use “My Own Entries” or “My List”, plus the school-mode setting. This stays in that browser and is never sent to us.
Chat, messaging or sharing between pupilsNone. Pupils cannot post, upload or message.
AI featuresOff in school mode. There is no generative AI for pupils to use.
Outside services contacted in school modeGitHub Pages (serves the site) and Cloudflare (supplies the live share prices behind a few of the largest fortunes). No pupil information is sent to either. As with any website, their standard server logs can include a device’s IP address.
Links to other sitesEach entry links to its Wikipedia page. These open only if a pupil taps them, and your normal school web filtering applies.

If your data protection officer has questions or wants to see more, email privacy@thedollarbook.com.

Ideas for lessons

These are suggestions to adapt, not a formal curriculum mapping. They suit maths, financial education, careers and PSHE or citizenship.

Maths · Financial education · 15–20 min

What do you really keep?

In the Career Guide, every job shows its salary in yellow and its take-home pay in blue, and opens to show exactly what the employer has to hold back. Pupils choose three jobs, work out how much is lost to tax and National Insurance in pounds and as a percentage, and compare their answers with the bands in the Tax tab. Switch the country to compare the UK with the US.

Maths · Number sense · 10 min

Guess the page

Before using the “Guess which page Elon Musk is on?” box, pupils estimate where he sits in a book that runs from $1 million to $1 trillion. Reveal the answer, then try other people. How many $ signs is one fortune? How many pages?

Maths · Large numbers · 20 min

How many years to earn it?

Pick a job from the Career Guide and a fortune from the top 20. Roughly how many years would the salary take to match the fortune? Write both numbers in standard form. Fortunes change daily, so treat the figure as a snapshot.

Maths · Tax · 20 min

Tax bands by hand

Choose a salary and work out the income tax using the Personal Allowance, Basic Rate and Higher Rate entries in the Tax section. Check it against the blue take-home figure. Why might the two differ slightly? (National Insurance and rounding.) Extension: the Tax Code entry explains the code on a payslip, such as 1257L, so pupils with a part-time job can check theirs.

Geography · Citizenship · 25 min

Rich country, rich people?

Each entry in Countries of the World shows a country’s GDP and its population. Pupils pick five countries from different continents, divide GDP by population to get GDP per person, rank them, and discuss why a country’s total GDP and its GDP per person can tell very different stories.

Careers · 15 min

Routes to a salary

Use the Career Guide and sort by highest pay. Find three jobs that pay above a chosen salary and compare the routes into them under “How to get in”. Which need a degree, and which are open through apprenticeships or training?

Media literacy · 15 min

Check the source

Pupils pick an entry, follow its source link and decide whether the figure is supported. Where could it be out of date? Pupils can send a correction request, which is a good way to see how sources and estimates work.

About the figures

  • Wealth figures are public estimates, mainly from Forbes and press reporting, and are not verified with the people concerned. They change constantly, and the largest fortunes update live from share prices.
  • Country figures (GDP and population) are rounded and drawn from IMF, World Bank, UN and national statistics. The GDP lists say which source and year each figure uses.
  • Take-home pay is simplified. It uses standard UK income tax and National Insurance rates for England, Wales and Northern Ireland, as set for the 2026/27 tax year and checked against GOV.UK in October 2026. It ignores Scottish Income Tax rates, pension contributions, student loans and benefits. Rates are reviewed each April, but please check HMRC before using it for exact calculations.
  • Salaries are typical UK averages or ranges, not what any one employer pays.

Spotted a mistake? Email privacy@thedollarbook.com with the subject “Correction Request”. We aim to review requests within 5 working days.

Using it on school devices

  • It works in any current browser on Chromebooks, Windows, Macs, iPads and phones. There is nothing to install.
  • To save it like an app, use the browser’s “Install” option (Chromebook, Windows) or Share → Add to Home Screen (iPad, iPhone). Open the school link first, so the app opens in school mode.
